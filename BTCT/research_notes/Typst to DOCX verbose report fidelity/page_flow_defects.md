## Key question 1: for each blank page, what is the last element before the section break, what are its properties, and what overflowed by how much

### Inferences
- The guard in `place_page` (`fits = end <= was + was_pending + 0.25 or end <= page_height - bottom_margin - 6`, convert.py around line 4136) cannot catch any of these: it models the page with `count = max(br lines, PDF rows)` so it never sees the extra line Word wraps, it stops at the first block without exact metrics (the sectPr paragraph), and the 0.7 pt tail paragraphs are modelled at face value. All six pages pass the guard and overflow by 0.02 to 2.4 pt.
- The blank pages are "pure" because what spilled was empty: Word needs the sectPr paragraph to fit on a page like any other, and a next-page section break right after it ends that page.

### Gaps
- Word's real line height for the unformatted sectPr paragraph was not measured (nothing is drawn on it); 12 to 16 pt is computed from the font metrics and the docDefaults 1.15 multiple.
- The "Content-Type" row height on data-a page 34 was not dumped; the 2.4 pt figure assumes the 12.6 to 12.8 pt rows seen everywhere else.

## Key question 2: one mechanism or several

### Takeaway
Three mechanisms, which combine on the same page: (1) pdf2docx's 1.0 pt dummy paragraph between consecutive one-row code tables adds about 1 pt per code line and the converter cannot take it back; (2) Word wraps some body paragraphs one line longer than typst (14.45 to 14.85 pt each) because its lines are 1.3% wider; (3) the section tail (0.7 pt paddings plus an unformatted sectPr paragraph) is the straw. Neither widow control, a non-splitting table row, a taller picture, nor the header/footer reservation played a part on any of the six pages.

### Cited Findings

Document-wide: 235 of 284 top-level tables in verbose-a are one-row one-cell (309 of 454 in data-a), with 186 dummy paragraphs of 20 twips (141 in data-a, 169 in data-b). ### Inferences
- The arithmetic closes: verbose-a p17 is 11 + 8 dummies plus one 14.45 pt re-wrap = 33.45 against +33.43 measured; p22 is 7 + 4 dummies plus one 14.85 pt line = 25.85 against +25.89; data-a p27 is 9 + 2 dummies plus 14.88 = 25.9 against +26.44. The small residue is the per-row `w:before` adjustments of the cell walk.
- The mechanisms are independent in origin (pdf2docx structure, Word's text metrics, pdf2docx's tail) but only their sum crosses the slack, which is why the spills cluster on pages that hold two code panels and a justified paragraph.
- The 1.3% width difference at 11 pt but not at 10 pt is unexplained; the one setting that differs between the two contexts in the typst source is justification (`#show table: set par(justify: false)`, main.typ line 105), and the one Word-side candidate is kerning (on in typst by default, off in Word without `w:kern`). Both are hypotheses.

### Gaps
- Why pdf2docx emits one table per code line rather than one table per rectangle was not traced into its table detection; the result (per-line `w:tbl` with exact rows that tile the rectangle) is what the docx shows.
- The cause of the width difference was not isolated (kerning versus justification versus hinting).

- Drift is a per-page phenomenon with three discrete sources; on pages without a code panel or a re-wrap it is rounding noise (under 1 pt). The 25 pt of extra body height that pdf2docx's measured margins give every page is what keeps 93% of the drifting pages from spilling.
- The spill pages are exactly the pages where the slack was already below 10 pt at the last text row (verbose-a 17: 7.2, 22: 6.7; data-a 27: 8.6) or where the next block was a whole exact row (data-a 32, 34; data-b 28).

### Gaps
- Sampling used text matching on the first 24 squashed characters; pages whose last row is inside a table with wrapped cells can be mismatched (verbose-d Word 20 reports a -79 pt step that was not verified by hand).

## Key question 5: which OOXML mechanisms could pin a page's content, which the converter is not using, and whether widow control is on

### Takeaway
Widow/orphan control is on everywhere (explicit `<w:widowControl/>` on 92 to 95% of paragraphs, and ISO 29500 makes it the default when unspecified) but was not a factor in any of the six spills. The converter already uses exact line spacing and exact row heights; it does not use `w:cantSplit`, `w:keepNext`, `w:keepLines`, `w:pageBreakBefore`, `w:framePr`, `w:tblpPr`, `w:docGrid` snapping, `w:doNotExpandShiftReturn` or `w:kern`. The fixes that follow from the measurements are structural (one table per panel, a formatted section tail, more line-fit relief) rather than pinning.

### Cited Findings
- ISO/IEC 29500 on `w:spacing`: "When determining the spacing between any two paragraphs, a consumer shall use the maximum of the inter-line spacing in each paragraph, the spacing after the first paragraph and the spacing before the second paragraph"; its own example reads `w:after="200" w:line="276" w:lineRule="auto"` as "at least 200 twentieths of a point after the last line" and "1.15 times (276 divided by 240) the normal single spacing", which is exactly the docDefaults every sectPr paragraph here inherits. [Microsoft Learn, SpacingBetweenLines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/documentformat.openxml.wordprocessing.spacingbetweenlines)
- ISO/IEC 29500 on `w:widowControl`: "If this setting is never specified in the style hierarchy, then this paragraph shall prevent a single line from being shown on a separate page whenever it would normally occur"; `<w:widowControl w:val="off"/>` turns it off. The generated styles.xml docDefaults and the Normal style carry no widowControl, and pdf2docx writes `<w:widowControl/>` on every text paragraph it creates (1760 of 1909 in verbose-a, 6949 of 7489 in data-a). [Microsoft Learn, WidowControl](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/documentformat.openxml.wordprocessing.widowcontrol); stats.py
- ISO/IEC 29500 on `w:cantSplit`: absent from the row and the table style, "this table row shall be allowed to split across multiple pages". No row in the six files carries it; the one-row code tables moved whole regardless because an exact-height single row has nothing to split. [Microsoft Learn, CantSplit](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/documentformat.openxml.wordprocessing.cantsplit)
- ISO/IEC 29500 on `w:doNotExpandShiftReturn`: "applications shall fully justify all lines in a paragraph when that setting is specified using the jc element except for the last line"; with the compat element present "any line which ends in a soft line break shall also not be fully justified". It is absent from every settings.xml, and 13 / 17 / 8 / 12 / 30 / 17 justified paragraphs per document contain a `<w:br/>`, which is why the sub-heading "11:13 UTC: Service discovery" on data-a Word page 27 is drawn as four words spread across the column at one baseline. ### Inferences
What a converter would have to write instead, per defect:
- Code panels: one `w:tbl` per rectangle with one `w:tr` per line, `w:trHeight w:hRule="exact"` equal to the per-line slice (the existing rows already tile the rectangle: 17.8 + 12.8 + ... + 15.7 = 160.4), and no paragraph between rows. That removes the 20-twip dummies entirely (11 pt on a 12-line panel) and lets `table_box_offset` match the rectangle's height so the table top lands on the PDF's. If per-line tables must stay, each row would have to be written 1.0 pt shorter than the PDF pitch (the cell paragraph's 8.55 pt floor allows it) or the dummy shrunk to the 14-twip minimum, which still leaves 0.7 pt per line.
- Re-wraps: the relief has to cover the measured 1.3% (6.6 pt on a 504 pt column, 15 pt at the 3% worst) rather than 4 pt, or the runs have to be narrowed (`w:spacing` in `w:rPr`, twentieths of a point per character, or `w:w` character scale in whole percent) or Word's kerning turned on with `w:kern` if that is the cause; the paragraphs the line-break pass declined (one `<w:br/>` after the sub-heading, none between body lines) need the same breaks; the 8 to 19 soft-hyphen line ends per document need a hyphen plus a break.
- Section tail: write `<w:spacing w:before="0" w:after="0" w:line="20" w:lineRule="exact"/>` and `<w:sz w:val="2"/>` on the sectPr paragraph and keep one 0.7 pt paragraph, so the tail costs about 1.7 pt instead of 6 pt of padding plus a 12 to 16 pt auto line plus 10 pt after. This alone would have left verbose-a 18 and 23 and data-a 28 on their pages (slack 3.6, 0.7 and 0.1 pt against a 1.7 pt tail, with the sectPr line being the only non-negotiable item and the padding paragraphs droppable).
- Guard: `place_page` should count the lines Word will draw (the re-wrap is visible from the line widths: 1.3% over the column) and include the tail in `end`, so a page that will overflow is refused or relieved rather than passed.
- Pinning: `w:tblpPr` (`w:vertAnchor="page" w:tblpY="<twips>"`) would fix a panel's top to the PDF's and `w:framePr` (`w:vAnchor="page" w:y="<twips>" w:hRule="exact"`) a paragraph's, which stops drift from accumulating, at the price of floating objects that text flows around and that editors cannot reflow; `w:keepNext`, `w:keepLines`, `w:cantSplit`, `w:pageBreakBefore` move whole blocks but pin nothing and would not have changed these six pages; `w:widowControl w:val="0"` removes a +1 line hazard on the day a paragraph straddles a page; `w:docGrid w:type="lines"` snaps every line to one pitch and cannot serve 12.69, 14.45 and 14.85 pt pitches on one page.
- The measured bottom margins (24.9 pt on most sections, against typst's 50.4 pt) are load-bearing: they are the slack that absorbs drift on every page that did not spill. Writing typst's margins would convert most pages with a code panel into spills until the dummies and re-wraps are gone.

### Gaps
- The ISO wording for how an `exact` line treats taller content was not fetched (the Learn enum page lists the values only); the converter's own 30-case measurement (baseline at 0.8 x line) is the operative fact used here.
- Whether `w:kern` closes the 1.3% is untested.
- Side observations outside page flow, recorded but not analysed: the per-line code tables on verbose-a page 17 carry no `w:shd` and Word draws no grey behind that code (visible in `va-17.png`), while the "Sharename" panel on page 22 carries pdf2docx's own `<w:shd w:fill="ebeceb"/>` on a `w:tcW 7240` cell, so the grey hugs the text; 9 pt code lines are 2.8% wider in Word (a long enough line could wrap inside its exact row and be clipped; not observed).
